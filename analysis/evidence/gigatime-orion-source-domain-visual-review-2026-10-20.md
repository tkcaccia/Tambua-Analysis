# Visual review of GigaTIME source-domain and ORION comparisons

Five fixed author-supplied H&E/COMET paired patches and all ten ORION registered patient windows were inspected in the saved contact sheets on 20 October 2026. The review is qualitative; the JSON reports contain the prespecified quantitative scores.

The five source-domain sheets show virtual DAPI concentrated over the author-provided nuclear regions. CD68 and CK are visibly informative in positive examples, but their targets are sparse. Several channels have zero or very few positive cells in a patch. The per-channel table therefore reports eligible patch count, average precision, and positive prevalence alongside AUROC. Some 256-pixel quadrant boundaries are visible in the direct author-notebook-style inference; these contacts are a checkpoint/input control, not a seamless whole-slide demonstration.

The ten ORION `*-paired.webp` sheets were reviewed for DAPI/Hoechst, CD68, CD4, CD8, CD20, PD-L1, CD3, PD-1, Ki67, and CK/Pan-CK. All 23 virtual channels were also reviewed in each `*-all23.webp` overview. Virtual DAPI generally follows the nuclear density, although brightness and nuclear detail differ. Many virtual protein maps follow broad tissue architecture rather than the sparse measured positive cells. This is especially clear for CD68 in CRC04, CRC19, and CRC23. There is no obvious hard rectangular shadow seam in these reviewed ORION overviews. The native-center details and original-resolution arrays remain available for closer inspection; a negative visual finding in a downsampled overview does not prove absence of a small artifact.

Measured signal is displayed magenta and virtual signal green. Display contrast uses independent per-image scaling and was never used for Spearman scoring. Source-domain AUROC against author cell targets and ORION raw-intensity Spearman assess different targets and cannot be subtracted or equated. The ORION ten-patient result does not validate virtual protein abundance or downstream cell types, even though it supports the physical input and nuclear-channel handling.
