# Tambua results

## MoNuSAC four-tissue, patient-separated evaluation

The [primary public gallery](https://tkcaccia.github.io/Tambua-Analysis/#matched-cohort) displays all 32 held-out images. The frozen cohort comprises 88 training ROIs from 40 patients and 32 test ROIs from 19 different patients. Breast (27/8), kidney (15/10), lung (27/4), and prostate (19/10) all occur in both training and test. Training patients appearing anywhere in the original test archive were removed before image selection. The source XML supplied physical pixel spacing; GigaTIME CNN inference used a strict 0.25 µm/pixel model grid. The 3 µm nucleus-anchored expansion is a proxy for cell-marker quantification, not expert-drawn cytoplasm.

All 120 ROI outputs have completion and validation records. The 83 new GPU cases completed in one authorized fail-fast Chiamaka bundle; 37 prior outputs were reused after decoded-image, mask, label and spacing equivalence checks. The final assigned GPU had no compute context and no owned runner process after completion. The [fixed scoring script](../analysis/score_monusac_matched.py) fitted one GitHub fastPLS 0.4 SIMPLS–LDA model on 23,213 annotated training cells and scored all 10,776 annotated test cells; no expert cell was missing either virtual-marker quantification table.

Pooled accuracy is **85.18%**, four-class balanced accuracy **42.09%**, macro F1 **43.15%**, and equal-weight patient accuracy **88.18%**. The training-majority baseline is **60.76%** accurate. Per-tissue accuracy: breast 85.83% (2,174 cells), kidney 84.59% (3,841), lung 95.75% (1,177), and prostate 81.95% (3,584). Epithelial recall is 6,173/6,548 (94.27%) and lymphocyte recall 3,005/4,178 (71.92%). Rare-class performance remains poor: macrophage 1/46 (2.17%) and neutrophil 0/4. Breast and lung test subsets lack some reference classes, so their four-class balanced accuracy is undefined. The [complete report](assets/monusac-matched/fastpls-report.json), [per-image confusion and DAPI checks](assets/monusac-matched/results.json), and [fitted model](assets/monusac-matched/fastpls-four-tissue.rds) are published. These are type predictions on expert-defined nuclear instances, not cell detection or independent biological validation of virtual markers.

## Archived exploratory MoNuSAC lung-to-prostate transfer

The [archived gallery](https://tkcaccia.github.io/Tambua-Analysis/#monusac) shows all ten held-out prostate ROIs alongside original H&E, expert cell-type masks, predicted masks, per-cell errors and GigaTIME virtual DAPI/CK/CD3/CD68. This result is **not** the requested matched-tissue test: fastPLS was trained on lung only, without prostate examples. GigaTIME CNN was run at 0.25 µm/pixel from original images with verified 0.19448–0.2527 µm/pixel metadata; its pinned revision is `a6556030bdb8071857559e12b921620aa1bbac5b`. The completed, fail-fast Chiamaka bundle has 37 individual completion checkpoints and no surviving owned GPU context. The train/test split is patient-disjoint: 27 lung ROIs/9 patients for training and 10 prostate ROIs/6 patients for testing.

CellPhenotyper quantified 21 virtual markers at each expert-drawn nuclear instance and 21 at a 3 µm expanded, nucleus-anchored **cell proxy**. All 5,243 training and 3,584 test expert cells matched both quantification tables. One fixed fastPLS 0.4 SIMPLS–LDA model was fitted on lung and applied to prostate. Its test accuracy is **0.8898** (3,189/3,584), balanced accuracy **0.4451**, macro F1 **0.4465** and equal-weight patient accuracy **0.8871**. The training-majority baseline is **0.5483** accurate. Epithelial recall is 0.9399 (1,847/1,965), lymphocyte recall is 0.8403 (1,342/1,597), and macrophage and neutrophil recall are both zero (20 and 2 expert cells). Consequently this is not evidence of reliable four-class recognition.

A preview-based top-decile DAPI enrichment screen passed in 8/10 test ROIs. The two failures remained in the fixed test score. Several virtual-DAPI previews are visually diffuse, including some that pass the coarse screen. An additional per-nucleus versus local-ring contrast is supplied for every image. These are spatial plausibility checks, not proof that DAPI or other markers are biologically faithful. Both predicted and expert type overlays color the **same expert nuclear geometries**; cell detection, segmentation, and full cell-boundary estimation were not scored. The [complete model report](assets/monusac-transfer/fastpls-report.json), [per-image confusion counts](assets/monusac-transfer/results.json) and [fitted fastPLS model](assets/monusac-transfer/fastpls-lung-to-prostate.rds) are available. The [reproduction script](../analysis/score_monusac_transfer.py) regenerated exactly the same 8,827 per-cell predictions from the saved completed image bundle. MoNuSAC source terms are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; the original full-resolution images are not committed.

## Calibrated ORION technical image result

The [results gallery](https://tkcaccia.github.io/Tambua-Analysis/) contains three fixed fields from one ORION CRC33_01 registered H&E window. Each field shows H&E, cyan contours of the released **computational** cell mask, GigaTIME DAPI over H&E, and the virtual DAPI, CK, CD3 and CD68 channels. The released mask was made by the ORION image-processing pipeline; it is **not** an expert-drawn cell-type annotation. It is used here for a spatial and identity handoff check only. The [ORION release](https://github.com/labsyspharm/orion-crc/blob/main/README.md) documents its registered images and generated masks.

The source field is 2048 × 2048 pixels, with OME metadata of 0.325 µm/pixel. CellPhenotyper resampled this exact physical field to 2663 × 2663 at 0.25 µm/pixel for GigaTIME CNN inference. The model snapshot is `a6556030bdb8071857559e12b921620aa1bbac5b` and the checkpoint SHA-256 is `d200308302c7fefd13b259c62de546f2c859c28d1079e9f67dc8808915d95d0c`. The [GigaTIME authors' geometry clarification](https://github.com/prov-gigatime/GigaTIME/issues/4) supports the 0.25 µm/pixel model grid.

The generated table contains exactly 4,104 positive cell IDs, all matching the published mask and measured-cell table, with no missing virtual-marker values. Twenty-three virtual channels are present and all scores are within 0–1. These are **uncalibrated virtual scores**, not measured protein concentrations or calibrated probabilities. The four preview JPEGs and table were generated in one bounded Chiamaka GPU case, with exit code 0 and no owned process or GPU compute context after completion.

The saved H&E/DAPI overlay was visually reviewed and DAPI signal coincided with many hematoxylin-stained nuclei. Among pixels inside published cell regions, the upper-quartile DAPI and hematoxylin signals overlapped in 381,114 pixels versus 184,256.9 expected under pixelwise independence, an enrichment of **2.068**. This is a morphological plausibility check; it is not expert nuclear-mask Dice. The original ORION dataset's registered H&E, published cell mask and measured-cell table can be reacquired via the [first-party release index](https://github.com/labsyspharm/orion-crc/blob/main/datarelease-README.md).

Within this single case, cell-level Spearman correlations between GigaTIME and ORION measured markers ranged from 0.599 for DAPI/Hoechst to −0.160 for CD20/CD20. The associations are stored in [machine-readable form](assets/orion-crc33-01/marker-associations.json) and reproduced with [`analysis/score_virtual_markers.py`](../analysis/score_virtual_markers.py). This evidence cautions against assuming every virtual channel is a reliable classifier input; it supplies no independent-patient cell-type accuracy estimate.

Two separately authorized CellViT++/HoVer-Net comparison bundles stopped before inference. In the first, the CellViT++ container lacked `pyvips` during image preparation. After a CPU-validated isolated overlay and new authorization, the second progressed to the CellViT++ reader, which rejected the temporary TIFF because its magnification metadata was absent. Both recorded CellViT++ exit code 1, verified that their owned process groups and GPU compute contexts were gone, and stopped before HoVer-Net. Neither bundle was retried. Consequently, **there are no matched baseline masks or scores to publish yet**. The website does not invent them. A biological comparison requires a calibrated, expert-typed test cohort with frozen patient-disjoint Tambua training, a fixed CellViT++ type mapping, and both baselines run on identical images. ORION's computational mask cannot be called expert annotation.

The [training-data provenance audit](DATA_PROVENANCE.md) distinguishes GigaTIME's published 21 in-house lung-adenocarcinoma training slides from this ORION colorectal technical case and the completed MoNuSAC expert test set. Neither selected source is reported as part of the GigaTIME translator training set. Exact sample-level exclusion for MoNuSAC requires the original training slide manifest, which is not published in the cited paper.
